Rustic Strings
An acoustic folk album cover, showing a guy sitting on a wooden porch at sunset playing his guitar, looking out over a quiet valley. I want to use heavy chiaroscuro lighting so the low sun casts harsh, warm orange highlights on him and long, dark shadows across the floorboards. To get that feeling of deep space, overlapp the porch beams in the front and let the background pine trees fade out into hazy blue-gray tones using atmospheric perspective. For motion and time, add some leaves blowing gently in the wind and the soft flicker of a small campfire down below. The color palette is super earthy—lots of deep ambers, rustic browns, and forest greens to keep it unified, but I threw in a bright neon-yellow lantern to break the pattern and add some variety. Finally,  set up an asymmetrical balance and messing with scale by making the guitarist way bigger in the foreground so he instantly catches your eye and anchors the whole scene

Value: Basically, value is just how light or dark something is. I use it to play around with shadows and highlights so things don't look flat and actually pop out with some real volume. 
Space: Space is how I fake depth on a flat 2D screen or paper, using tricks like making things overlap, shrinking distant objects, or making the background hazy. 
Unity: Unity is basically the glue of the design—it's making sure everything matches and feels like it belongs together instead of looking like a messy pile of random stuff. 
Balance: Balance is how you spread out the visual weight so the whole piece doesn't feel like it's tipping over to one side, whether it's perfectly symmetrical or unevenly weighted. 
Rhythm: Rhythm is how you lead the viewer's eyes around the canvas using repeating shapes, lines, or patterns so they naturally follow a certain flow.
